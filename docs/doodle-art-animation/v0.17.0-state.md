# v0.17.0 state (2026-09-23)

The paper foundation: build step A of the canvases plan (`/mnt/project-files/canvases/plan.md`, section 4.1). Films
could only use two papers, the cream notebook page and the night page. This release turns those into the first
entry of a paper registry, so new papers (blueprint, lab, chalk, codex, toned) can be added as files without touching
the engine again. It adds no new paper itself; those are builds B and C.

This version also ships the narration track (PR #15, merged to main before this one without its own version
number); `references/sound.md`, "Narration", and `tests/doodle-art-animation/narration_test.py` cover it.

## What changed

- **Paper registry.** `defineGround(name, spec)` registers a paper: its tone (light or dark world), its texture
  builder, palette, vignette, grain strength, topographic loops, header sound, optional treatment and contrast
  floors. `definePaperSet(name, { light, dark })` pairs two. `notebook` (`cream` + `night`) is built by exactly the
  code that built the old textures, and stays the default.
- **Story and plate options.** `defineStory({ paper })` picks a set; `plate.paper` changes one plate (a set, or a
  single paper, which then decides the plate's world). Each plate draws with its own paper current: `drawPlate()`
  and `renderFrame()` swap the plate's palette fields into `PAL` and put them back afterwards, so the two plates of a
  transition can sit on different papers. A notebook film swaps nothing.
- **Every paper lookup follows the plate's paper.** `background`, the glyph halos (`haloGround`, `haloInk`),
  `backing`, `insetLens`, `bgTex`, the transitions' paper textures, the frame fill, the vignette, the grain strength,
  the lens-in anticipation ring and the header sound. About twenty hard-coded HUD and transition colours moved into
  `PAL` fields (`lens`, `lensEdge`, `nightCurtain`, `rim`, `pageBack`, `counter`, `dialShadow`, …) with the same
  values. The `legible()` and halo caches are keyed by paper.
- **Lazy textures at the film's size.** Only the papers a film uses are built, at `W × H`, so the portrait work can
  land on either side of this change. `TEX.paper` / `TEX.night` / `TEX.vigPaper` / `TEX.vigNight` still work.
- **Treatment layer.** A paper with a `treatment` gets its plate's paper, scene, reticle and overlay drawn on a layer,
  treated (`tooth`, `filter`, `color`, `glow`, or a function), and composited; the HUD goes on top untreated. HUD
  glyph halos use the treated texture so they stay invisible.
- **`build.py` includes papers.** Files in `toolkit/grounds/` are included when the story names one of their papers
  or sets; `_*.js` files always. An unknown paper name gives a warning at build time and a clear error at boot.
- **Specimen and tests.** `story_swatch.js` puts every paper on screen; `smoke_test.py` builds it and checks each
  paper's contrast through `window.__paperProbe()` (ink 7, labels 4.5, accent 3, or the paper's `minContrast`).
  The notebook's vermilion measures 2.5:1 against the cream texture, so `cream` keeps a lowered accent floor of 2.5.
- **Docs.** `api.md` has a **Paper** section; `style.md` has a "Papers" section with the texture rules for
  re-encoded short-form video.

## How it was checked

- Every second frame of all nine stories (the seven bundled ones plus the two real films, Dressed in Mucus and
  Pressed for Time) was hashed on v0.16.1 and on this build. Six stories matched in every frame. In three (One Drop,
  brushes, seams) one browser page per run gave different hashes; re-rendering those frames matched, and the v0.16.1
  engine shows the same page-to-page noise on the same frames, so it comes from the test harness (most likely font
  loading on one page), not the engine. Median draw time per frame moved by 0–4 ms.
- A temporary blueprint-like paper and a kraft-like paper with every treatment kind were built into the swatch:
  no page errors, transitions across papers render, and their probes cleared the contrast floors. Treated frames
  cost about 100 ms more per frame at 1080p with glow on, which builds B and C should watch.
- `smoke_test.py` on the swatch and One Drop passes.

## Known limits

- The treatment runs per plate per frame; a glow at 1080p is the costliest part.
- `eraseOut` and `journeyPath` fill with the flat paper colour, which shows on a strongly textured paper.
- Per-paper header sounds other than `scratch` and `readout` (chalk tap, marker squeak) are left to the sound work.
