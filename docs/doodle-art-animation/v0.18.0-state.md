# v0.18.0 state (2026-09-28)

Closed captions for narrated films. Kosta asked for "closed caption support ... with different backgrounds, fonts,
font animations etc." Before this, a narrated film only got a sidecar `film.srt` and a plain white caption overlay in
the HTML player. Now captions come in three forms, and a film without narration is unaffected.

## What changed

- **A subtitle track inside the MP4, by default.** Every full render of a narrated film still writes `film.srt`,
  and now also copies the same captions into the MP4 as a `mov_text` subtitle track (language `eng`, off until the
  viewer turns it on). The video and audio are copied, not encoded again. `--no-cc-track` leaves the track out.
- **Captions drawn into the picture, on request.** `render.mjs --captions [style]` draws them into every frame
  (burned-in, for social media or muted playback). No track is added then, since the words are already on screen.
  A style name that does not exist stops the render and lists the styles.
- **Eight named styles**, built from five fonts, six backgrounds and six text animations:

  | style | font | background | animation |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `notebook` (default) | Inter Tight | paper halo | fade |
  | `scrap` | Fraunces | torn paper scrap | rise |
  | `tape` | Patrick Hand | masking tape strip | words pop in |
  | `marker` | Inter Tight | yellow highlighter | spoken word highlighted |
  | `margin` | Caveat | paper halo | types on |
  | `field` | mono | torn paper scrap | types on |
  | `broadcast` | Inter Tight | dark band | cut |
  | `social` | Inter Tight, larger | dark outline | words pop in |

  Any part can be changed on the command line (`--captions "style=tape,anim=rise,pos=top"`) or in the story
  (`defineStory({ captions: { style, font, bg, anim, size, pos, burn } })`). A plate moves them with
  `captions: { pos: 'top' }` or hides them with `captions: false`. Text colour follows the paper (ink on light
  pages, cream on dark ones), and the accent colour for the highlighted word is checked for contrast.
- **Better caption splitting.** Captions used to be filled greedily up to 84 characters, which could strand one word
  on a line or split "20 °C" across two lines. Now a long sentence is cut into captions of at most two lines of
  42 characters, cut at sentence ends and commas first, never between a number and its unit, never
  after "a", "the" or "of", and with lines of similar length. Captions follow each other without flicker (gaps under
  half a second are closed), and each stays up at least 0.8 s.
- **`captions.replace`** rewrites spoken words for the reader, for example "forty-five degrees Celsius" to "45 °C",
  inside the same timing.
- **Word times.** When `voice.json` has word times (ElevenLabs), a caption starts on its first word exactly, and the
  word animations follow the words. `voice.mjs` now keeps those times; `build.py` passes them through. Gemini gives
  no word times, so they are estimated by syllables.
- **A check for captions that cover things.** `text_check.mjs --captions [style]` draws the captions in and prints a
  `CAPTION` line for each plate whose caption block (background included) covers other text: a bottom card, an axis
  label, the stage dial. On Glass Transition it found three plates (IV, V and VI) where bottom captions sit on a
  label or a temperature bar, which is also what the stills showed. `doodle-render` and `film-reviewer` run it before
  a burned-in render is delivered.
- **The player.** The HTML player draws captions on the canvas in the film's style: `c` turns them on and off, `v`
  tries the next style.
- **Fonts.** The two handwriting faces (Patrick Hand, Caveat) load only when a caption style uses them, so films
  without them load exactly the fonts they did before. `--fonts local` embeds them from `@fontsource/patrick-hand` and
  `@fontsource/caveat` when those are installed.
- **Docs and reviewers.** `references/render.md` ("Captions"), `references/api.md`, `references/intake.md`,
  `SKILL.md` step 7 and `doodle-render` describe the forms and when to make a `film_cc.mp4`. `film-reviewer` checks
  that burned-in captions do not cover a card, a label or the hero; `audio-reviewer` knows about the subtitle track.

## How it was checked

- New `tests/doodle-art-animation/captions_test.py` (run in CI): line breaks and caption length on a long
  sentence, the number-and-unit rule, `captions.replace` in the page and in the `.srt`, exact word timing against
  estimated timing, each of the eight styles drawing into the bottom band only, `pos=top`, a plate's `captions: false`
  and `pos`, word and type reveals, frames that do not depend on the frame drawn before, the script face loading at
  boot, and `render.mjs` itself (the style line, a bad style, the subtitle track via ffprobe, no track when burned in
  or with `--no-cc-track`, and a film without narration).
- `captions_test.py` also checks that `text_check --captions` reports a caption over a label, and only with
  `--captions`.
- `voice_test.mjs` has a check that `voice.json` keeps each word's times.
- All eight styles were drawn on the Salt in Water example and on Glass Transition, on cream and night plates, and
  looked at. A sample clip of all eight on Glass Transition is in `/mnt/project-files/captions/`.
- `acceptance_check.py` (new item C1) passes its static checks.

## Known limits

- With Gemini narration the word animations (`words`, `highlight`) follow estimated word times, so a highlighted
  word can be a little early or late on long words.
- `--fonts local` needs the two caption font packages installed for the `tape` and `margin` styles; without them
  the render warns and uses the Google Fonts link.
- Burned-in captions sit at the bottom by default and can cover a bottom card. `text_check --captions` finds these,
  but the fix is by hand: move the plate's captions (`captions: { pos: 'top' }` or a baseline y) or the text. On a
  busy plate neither top nor bottom may be clear, so the text itself has to move. `text_check` sees text only, not
  art, so a caption over the hero is still found by looking at stills.
- The subtitle track is `mov_text`, which QuickTime, VLC and most browsers show. Some social sites ignore embedded
  tracks; upload the `.srt` there, or use a burned-in render.
