# v0.16 state (2026-09-22)

Branch `doodle-v0.16-speed`. v0.16 is a speed release. It came from renders in Claude cloud sessions, which run on
4-core machines with no GPU: the render skill's worker rule picked 2 workers there, and the QA checks each used one
core while three sat idle. Everything below was measured on one of those machines (4 Xeon vCPUs, 15 GB), with the
bundled One Drop film (55 s, 1,320 frames) unless it says otherwise. The new item is P1 in `ACCEPTANCE.md`.

## What changed

- **Workers: one per core.** `render.mjs` now uses one page per CPU core, at most 8, when `--workers` is not
  given, and `doodle-render` no longer subtracts two cores. Headless Chromium draws the canvas on the CPU, one page
  per core, so fewer pages than cores leaves cores idle. Drawing One Drop: 187 s with 2 workers (the old rule on 4
  cores), 105 s with 4. A render uses about 95% of the CPU with 4 workers and 54% with 2.
- **Encoder preset: `medium` instead of `slow`.** At `--bitrate 3800k` the encode took longer than the drawing:
  119 s with `slow`, 65–68 s with `medium`. Same file size (the bitrate sets it), SSIM 0.658 against 0.660 and PSNR
  30.9 against 31.1 dB versus the source frames, and 200% crops that look the same. `--preset slow` still works.
- **QA sets on several pages.** `--stills`, `--sheet`, `--strips`, `--seams` and `--sheet-range` spread their
  frames over up to 4 pages (or `--workers`), each page taking at least 6 frames. Contact sheet 17.7 → 8.1 s,
  strips 34.8 → 13.4 s, seams 20.0 → 9.7 s, an 8 s range sheet 12.1 → 6.6 s. The images are byte-identical to the
  one-page ones, except the two sheets of One Drop's pan seam (see the fix below).
- **`text_check` 8× faster.** It only needs the text calls, not the pixels, but it read one pixel after every frame
  to keep Chromium from stalling, and that made Chromium draw the whole frame. It now throws the frame's queued
  drawing away (`ctx.reset()`) and reads frames on up to 4 pages (`--workers`), then follows the lines in film order
  exactly as before. One Drop 148 → 17.5 s, The Long Release 103 → 11.8 s, the gallery 95 → 12.2 s, JSON identical.
- **`legibility_check` about 3× faster.** It needs the pixels, so it keeps drawing, but on up to 4 pages
  (`--workers`). One Drop 45 → 16 s, The Long Release 38 → 15 s, the gallery 29 → 12 s, JSON identical.
- **Fix: a frame no longer depends on the frame drawn before it.** Spreading QA frames over pages showed that One
  Drop's whip pan came out slightly differently depending on which frame the page had drawn before. The pan's two
  sheets meet at a sub-pixel seam, and the previous frame showed through that one column (324–342 pixels, up to 5
  levels out of 255), because nothing cleared the canvas between frames. So a full render with 4 workers and one
  with 6 differed there. `renderFrame` now fills the canvas with the plate's paper colour first; the frames it
  changes are only those with a gap, and only in the gap.
- **P1 in the acceptance checks:** the defaults above as text checks, and in `--full` every third drawing of every
  transition in One Drop, The Long Release and the transition reel drawn after two different predecessors.

## Measured, whole run

| Step, One Drop on 4 cores | v0.15 (2 workers, `slow`) | v0.16 (defaults: 4 workers, `medium`) |
|---|---|---|
| Drawing the frames | 199 s | 112 s |
| Encoding the MP4 (`--bitrate 3800k`) | 120 s | 69 s |
| Whole render | 321 s | 184 s |
| The rendering QA commands in `doodle-qa` (sheet, strips, seams, `text_check`, `legibility_check`) | 249–265 s | 65 s |

Both MP4s are 24 MB and 1,320 frames, `motion_check` median 1.70 (v0.15) and 1.72 (v0.16), 0% still, and
`audio_check` passes both. The old and the new engine drew the frames in the same time (110.9 s and 109.3 s with the
same `render.mjs`), so the paper fill costs nothing measurable, and their JPEG frames are byte-identical except the
16 frames of the pan (146–161), the ones the fix changes.

## Known limits

- Chromium itself can still shift a frame by 1 level out of 255 depending on what the page drew before (a `roll`
  frame drawn before a `lensIn` frame on the same page shifts the lens-in frame by at most 1 level over most of it).
  That is below what JPEG keeps and is not the engine's state; P1 allows it.
- Where the time goes in one frame (One Drop, one page): about 60 ms of JavaScript, 90–140 ms of Chromium drawing the
  queued canvas commands, and about 55 ms of JPEG encoding at quality 0.95. The gate weave (the whole frame drawn
  with a sub-pixel shift and turn) costs about a third of the drawing; simplifying it would change the look, so it
  stays. The glyph halo's paper copy (`haloGround`, redrawn for every plate on every frame) costs about 15% of the
  frame. The two frames of one drawing could share it without changing a pixel, but that is a cache inside the
  engine and was left for a later release.
- At 3800k both presets lose most of the paper grain; that is the bitrate, not the preset.
