## Principles
- Build every sound as breath (quiet 80-150 ms upbeat), bloom (300-400 ms body), tail (transparent fade into the room); the upbeat warns instead of startling [7].
- Keep sounds at the edge of attention and short: taps 30-150 ms, toggles 80-200 ms, confirms 300-500 ms, nothing but hero moments over about 1.5 s; silence is a design choice and frequent actions get none [1][6][7][10][11].
- Use few sounds, one family: same envelope shapes, same register band, same two or three techniques; vary pitch and rhythm inside the family (Windows went from about 40 sounds to 8) [6][12][13].
- Mirror pairs carry meaning: rising and settling = success, falling and dull = error, a dissonant dyad (minor 9th) = "look here"; keep errors dull, not harsh [6][9][10].
- Avoid sci-fi cliches and classic synth or sampled-instrument signatures; build an original timbre (tuned noise, beating sines, additive stacks) that feels native to the product [4][5][6].
- Sound follows the animation: peak on the keyframe where motion lands, length equals move length, bigger objects are lower and slower, secondary layers offset by a few frames and in different frequency bands, nudge 1-2 frames until it feels locked [14][15][23].
- Derive envelopes from the real animation curve (easing velocity, scale, position) so the pitch or gain "overshoots" when the object overshoots [12][14].
- Risers must end exactly on the event and resolve into a hit, a sub drop or a brief silence; reversed tails give the same lift without a synth sweep [17][18][20].
- Let pitch and rate encode data: higher pitch for more, faster clicks for more, stepped bands (about 5 % per step) read better than a smooth slide [12][27][28].
- Keep notes at least about 80 ms (30 ms for one or two note cues), pitches inside about 130 Hz to 5 kHz, accent the first note, lengthen the last, leave 0.1 s between cues [13].
- Glitch only with one committed error type (stutter of 10-30 ms slices, or bit reduction), never random mush [26].
- End cards and stingers: 1-3 s, 2-5 notes, one hero timbre plus a texture layer, roll the low end off so laptop speakers stay clean [29].
- Sounds must survive repetition, so seed small per-play variation (pitch +-2 %, level +-1.5 dB, timing +-8 ms) and never repeat identical samples back to back [7].

Conventions: levels are dBFS peak targets for a quiet product-video mix; "key" means the beat's scale (root, fifth, ninth are safe); "IR" means a synthetic impulse response built in code (exponentially decaying filtered noise) for a ConvolverNode.

## Shortlist for 3dlight
1. siliconeTap - the root of a new UI family: matte, pitched into the key, clearly not the old pops, ticks or glass mallets.
2. breathBloom - the three-stage cue (breath, bloom, tail) gives every beat boundary one consistent, calm, non-whoosh signature.
3. tunedSweep - replaces noise whooshes with an airy, chord-tuned pass that sounds premium and original.
4. haloHit - matte thud plus a beating halo (no bells) for headline and hero-object reveals.
5. overshootPop - pitch and gain follow the real easing curve, so labels feel physically locked to the motion.
6. staggerCascade - one soft tap per item climbing the scale for word, card and list reveals, with the last note lengthened.
7. lensRack - two beating tones locking into one is a clear, sober "coming into focus" for zooms and highlights.
8. softLatch - precise two-stage matte click for capsules closing and parts docking; pairs with airOpen.
9. oximeterBeep - a pitch-coded clinical monitor beep gives the biotech film its own recognisable data sound.
10. shepardRise - an endless rise with no peak that bridges the 45 s loop seam without a bump.
