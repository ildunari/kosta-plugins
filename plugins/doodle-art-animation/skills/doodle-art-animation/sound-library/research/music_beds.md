# Music beds, musical palettes and rhythm for calm premium science/tech video (3dlight)

Scope: what music and sonic branding sound like in biotech/medtech films, booth loops, big-tech product and keynote audio, SaaS films, "corporate minimal" library music, science documentary beds and pulse-based minimalism, turned into Web Audio building blocks. Time grid used throughout: 45 s loop, 10 beats of 4.5 s. Reference key D (D3 = 146.83 Hz); change `tonic` freely, voicings are given as note names so they transpose.

## Principles
- Identity is timbre first, melody second: Intel's mnemonic was recognised 100% with its real sound vs 80% as a violin melody, and it was blended xylophone/marimba. So 3dlight needs its own palette (FM glass, additive sine pads, soft square, chord-tuned air, tuned clicks) and should avoid marimba, felt, kalimba and music box, which are the doodle identity. [7]
- Round, not sharp: Windows 11 "rounded" its sounds to match rounded UI and spans 250 to 8000 Hz so it stays audible. Use attacks of 2 ms or more, no raw saw above 3 kHz, bed energy mostly 150 Hz to 6 kHz. [5]
- One tonal home and rare hero sounds: Google builds the whole soundscape on one note and chord, keeps the hero under 1.5 s and infrequent, and makes frequent sounds quieter. One tonic for bed, SFX and stingers; at most one hero per section. Progress can be harmonic: IV with 2 voices, V with 3, I with 4. [3, 4]
- Minimalism recipe: constant pulse, consonant diatonic chords with slow harmonic rhythm, texture added one layer at a time, phasing and additive cells. Reich moves between chords by re-voicing, inversion or relative minor, keeping common tones. [1, 12]
- Make phasing loop-safe: Eno used incommensurable loop lengths; in a 45 s loop choose periods 45/n (pairwise coprime n) so everything realigns exactly at the seam, and cell lengths that divide 320 sixteenths (1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 16, 20, 32, 40...). The existing doodle `phasing` periods do not tile 45 s. [2, 12, 36]
- Tempo and mood: tech/biotech library beds sit at 90 (biotech bed) and 100 to 140 BPM; slow plus major is a mixed cue that reads calm-positive, not sad. Grid options: 106.67 BPM (2 bars per beat), 53.33 BPM (1 bar per beat), 80 BPM in 3/4 (bar = 2.25 s, the same bar length as 106.67 in 4/4). [10, 11, 28]
- Mix order is effects over music: bed 8 to 12 dB under SFX peaks, carve the bed where cues live, duck it a few dB for the cue plus about 0.5 s each side; booth halls are loud, so cues must read by transient and midrange, not sub. [20, 32]
- Low notes need harmonics: small speakers drop 55 to 100 Hz, so give bass notes 2f and 3f components (missing fundamental) instead of more sub level. [18]
- Sidechain pumping is a pad effect with tempo-synced release and a low depth (1.5 to 4 dB); Web Audio has no sidechain input, so schedule the gain curve on a dedicated duck node. [22, 35]
- Pluck and bell recipes: inharmonic carrier:modulator ratios (1:1.4, 1:3.5) are metallic, a modulator with fast decay and zero sustain gives the pluck strike, index envelope falling to near 0 leaves a pure sine; pluck by short envelope-driven low-pass and a short gate. [13, 14, 23]
- Shimmer and grains: shimmer = octave-up tail fed back into the reverb, kept at low feedback with a low-passed return and sparse material; because every note is synthesised, a delayed octave-up re-synthesis does the same job deterministically. Grains are 1 to 100 ms. [16, 17]
- Voicing: wide intervals at the bottom, tight at the top, no thirds in the low register; bass root folded into 55 to 110 Hz. [26]

| bus | rule |

| everything except bass | HP 90 Hz; bass and sub mono below 120 Hz |

### Bed A: Glass Pulse (pulse minimalism, premium biotech)
- Tempo and harmony: 106.67 BPM, 4/4, reichCycle5 in D, two beats (4 bars) per chord: Dmaj9, F#m9, Bm9, Gmaj7#11, Asus2(add4).
- Instruments: glassPad voiced by openVoicer (-8 dB); subPulse on chord root at steps 0 and 6 (-6 dB); gatedArp with fmGlassPluck on a 5-sixteenth cell [1, 5, 9, 3, 5'] at 0 dB; sineClick E(7,16) at -26 dB; metalHat closed on steps 2, 6, 10, 14 at -28 dB (beats 5-7 only); phantomKick quarter pump 2.5 dB on pad and sub; dottedPing 0.4219 s on the arp at -14 dB; tunedAir at -34 dB; shimmerTail on each chord change at -18 dB; matteGlue on the bus.
- Arc over 10 beats: 1 pad + air; 2 + subPulse; 3 + sineClick; 4 + arp (one note of the cell, adding one per beat up to 5); 5 + metalHat + dottedPing; 6 peak; 7 clicks out; 8 hats out, arp cell thins to 3 notes; 9 arp out; 10 pad + air + loopSeam, matching beat 1.

## Shortlist for 3dlight
1. fmGlassPluck - the signature timbre; glassy and cold, nothing like marimba, felt or kalimba, and the voice for arps, pings and stingers.
2. glassPad - additive sine pad replaces the doodle's warm saw pad and gives every bed its clean, transparent ground.
3. phantomKick - sidechain pulse with no audible kick, so the rhythm no longer reuses the soft kick/rim/shaker.
4. sineClick - tuned digital clicks give a new rhythmic identity that is also harmonic and stays out of SFX noise space.
5. gatedArp - Reich/Glass-style pulse engine on the 5-sixteenth cell, which tiles the 45 s loop exactly.
6. subPulse - round sine bass with 2f/3f so it still reads on booth and laptop speakers.
7. shimmerTail - cheap deterministic octave-up halo that makes chord changes and reveals feel premium.
8. reichCycle5 - five-chord I iii vi IV Vsus cycle that fits 10 beats and returns cleanly; replaces I-vi-IV-V-ii-V.
9. octaveFlam - tiny hero ping (under 1.4 s) for title and loop start, following Google's Super G logic.
10. loopSeam - reversed-bell breath plus tail folding so the 45 s loop is inaudible at the seam.
