## Principles
- Cue each sound to a physical event and land the onset on the frame of contact or a few ms after; sound that leads picture is noticed sooner than sound that lags (keep early error under ~15-35 ms) [26].
- Keep molecular events local and small. Binding forces act only at close range and most collisions are unproductive, so approach sounds stay near-silent, the full sound lands on the one productive contact, and extra instances are tiny [3].
- Borrow the visual physics for the sound. Diffusion is a random walk (random pitch steps and pan, not glides) [3]; many-particle sounds are Poisson events with decaying energy [15]; a dense stream of tiny events fuses into a texture [16]; crumple-like noise uses a power-law amplitude spread [14].
- Material lives in decay time more than pitch: use one modal bank and change its T60 and mode ratios (glass long and inharmonic, ceramic short and dry, steel mid, silicone no ring) [10, 11, 12].
- Wet and soft means air: add a small squish or splash layer, shape with pressure-over-time envelopes, and finish with a pulled-out "suck" or tiny bubbles. Bubble pitch is inverse to radius, 10-20 ms chirps [7, 8, 9].
- Let sound carry data, but never pitch alone: use density (events per second), tremolo or brightness as a second channel, and a fixed reference tone if the viewer must judge a target. Mappings are hard to read without the picture, so pair them with the visual [5, 21].
- Small = high, bright, short; large = low, dark, long. Drive bubble, vesicle and sphere pitches from on-screen size, and put pitched accents in the film's key [9, 25].
- For a calm clinical look: soft, careful, non-alarming, no harsh beeps, no tense drones; one consistent identity across sounds; frequent sounds simple and understated, rare hero sounds richer [17, 18, 19, 20].
- Give each scale its own space. Berry's practice is a distinct soundscape per subject and large reverb for scale [1]; generic scoring is the usual complaint about biology films [28]. Molecule = dry and close, cell = short room, tissue = longer, softer room.
- Body sounds read as filtered, low, close: think "underwater" lowpass, bone-and-tissue resonance, and pulsatile flow rather than literal heart recordings [23, 24, 22].
- Layer transient + body + tail, vary every play from the seeded RNG, and keep 3-5 variants for anything cued more than three times [30].
- Use silence and sparseness; a quiet bed with a few exact events looks more premium than constant sound [32].

## Shortlist for 3dlight
1. siliconeTap - the new "matte" identity: soft pitched size-aware taps for spheres and cells that replace sine pops and ticks.
2. dockSeat - the hero binding event, quiet approach then one exact seat on the contact frame, with no glassy ring.
3. bondLock - two beating tones lock to one; a tonal accent that tells "binding" without piano or mallets.
4. diffuseDrift - a Brownian grain cloud for diffusion and penetration beats; a new texture with no bubbles or noise whoosh.
5. accrete - taps fuse into a silk sheen for coating and layer-by-layer beats, built from density rather than a swell.
6. dopplerFlow - a clinical, pulsing bed on the beat grid that replaces kick and shaker rhythm with blood-flow character.
7. stokesGlow - excitation blip then a lower blooming glow for fluorescence; brightness rather than loudness, distinct from glass mallets.
8. capsuleOpen - dry shell click, powder breath and granules; a premium pharma object sound.
9. releaseCurve - grain density follows the release curve, so the sound mirrors the plotted data line.
10. engulf - a soft, filtered swallow for endocytosis and cell-wrap beats, wet without being gross.
